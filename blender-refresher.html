TO THE FORGE

A refresher built around your background. You took landscape design and character modeling courses — meaning your spatial instincts and mesh fundamentals are intact. This guide skips what you already know and focuses on what's different when you're designing for physical output on a K2 Plus, specifically tabletop terrain and miniatures.
Mindset

DESIGNING FOR PHYSICS, NOT PIXELS

Your game design courses optimized for render engines and game engines — things like poly count, UV maps, texture baking. For 3D printing, those concerns flip almost entirely. You're now designing for gravity, layer adhesion, and wall strength. The mesh has to exist in the real world.

🎮
Game Design Thinking
Low poly counts. UV unwrapping matters. Normals for lighting. Quads preferred. No volume needed — just visible surface.
🖨️
Print Design Thinking
Watertight mesh required. Walls must have thickness. Overhangs under 45°. No floating geometry. Volume is everything.
🏔️
Your Landscape Background
Translates directly to terrain. You already think in ground planes, elevation, organic form. Dungeon floors, rocky outcrops, hills — this is familiar territory.
🧙
Your Character Background
Translates to minis, creatures, NPCs. The challenge is scale — 28–32mm means details you model at 10× scale need to survive being shrunk. Thin swords snap. Beefy designs survive.
⚠️
The single biggest mental shift: in game design, floating geometry and open meshes are fine. For printing, if your mesh isn't "watertight" — completely sealed with no holes or open edges — your slicer either fails or produces garbage. Every surface must connect to form a closed volume.
Setup

FIRST THINGS TO CONFIGURE

Before touching a mesh, get Blender configured for physical output. These settings will save you from scale nightmares when you import into your slicer.

1
Set Units to Millimeters
Scene Properties (camera icon) → Units → Unit System: Metric → Length: Millimeters → Unit Scale: 0.001. This makes 1 Blender unit = 1mm. Without this, your "28mm mini" imports into your slicer at 28 meters.
2
Enable the 3D Print Toolbox Add-on
Edit → Preferences → Add-ons → search "3D Print Toolbox" → enable it. This becomes your pre-flight scanner. Run it before every export — it catches non-manifold edges, thin walls, and overhang problems before they waste a print.
3
Set Overlays: Face Orientation
In the Viewport Overlays dropdown, enable Face Orientation. Blue faces = normals pointing outward (correct). Red faces = normals flipped inward (will cause print failures). You want an all-blue model.
4
Know Your Scale References
28mm = standard DnD mini height (feet to eye). 32mm = "heroic" scale (more popular now). Warhammer 40K is 28–32mm "heroic." Add a reference cube of exactly 28×10×10mm to every new project until scale becomes muscle memory.
💡
CFS Color Tip: Your K2 Plus CFS system splits colors based on Z-height or painted face assignments in your slicer — but you control the geometry that those color regions land on. If you want a sword to be silver and a scabbard to be brown, they need to be separate mesh objects that you assign colors to in the slicer. Plan your color splits in Blender before you export.

Workflows

TERRAIN VS MINIS — TWO DIFFERENT APPROACHES

Your landscape and character backgrounds map directly to these two workflows. They use different tools and different thinking.

🏰
Hard Surface Terrain
Dungeon walls, buildings, architectural models, ruins.

Workflow: Block modeling → Boolean operations → Bevel for edges → Modular design (pieces that lock together).

Key tools: Extrude, Boolean modifier, Array modifier for repeating elements, Mirror modifier.

Design rule: Make it modular. A 4×4" floor tile that connects to walls on all 4 sides is worth 10× a one-off piece.
🐉
Organic Minis & Creatures
DnD characters, monsters, Warhammer units.

Workflow: Sculpt mode for forms → Remesh for clean topology → Detail passes → Decimate for file size.

Key tools: Sculpt brushes (Draw, Clay, Smooth, Crease), Voxel Remesh, Dyntopo.

Design rule: Thick limbs survive. Thin sword = snapped sword. Design for the print, not the concept art.
🎯
Start with scatter terrain. Small, quick pieces — barrels, crates, bones, campfires, rubble piles. Each one takes 30–60 min to model, prints in under 2 hours, and sells at $8–15 each. This is how you rebuild your Blender instincts fast with real output to show for it.
🚫
Common mistakes coming from game design: Leaving meshes open (not watertight). Using subdivisions for organic shapes without applying them before export — high-poly sculpts need to be decimated. Forgetting to apply the Mirror modifier before STL export. Modeling at the wrong scale and not catching it until the slicer.
Pre-flight

BEFORE YOU HIT EXPORT

Run this every time. One skipped step costs you a failed print and hours of reprinting. Click each item as you go.

Export Checklist — Every Single Model
Units set to mm — Scene Properties → Units → Millimeters, scale 0.001
Apply all transforms — Select all, Ctrl+A → Apply All Transforms
Apply all modifiers — Mirror, Solidify, Array, Subdivision must be applied not just visible
Merge by Distance — Edit mode, Select All (A), M → Merge by Distance. Removes duplicate verts
Recalculate Normals — Alt+N → Recalculate Outside. Check Face Orientation overlay — all blue
Run 3D Print Toolbox — Zero non-manifold edges, zero intersecting faces, wall thickness above 1.2mm
Verify dimensions — Press N, check Item panel. Is the model actually the size you want in mm?
Export STL — File → Export → STL. Check "Scene Unit" box. Binary format. Selection Only if needed
Re-import STL to verify — Import your own export back into Blender. Does it look right at the right size?
Test slice at 20–30% scale first — Always run a small test before committing hours of print time

Projects

YOUR FIRST 3 BUILDS — IN ORDER

These are sequenced to rebuild your Blender muscle memory while producing actual sellable output.

1
Scatter Terrain: Barrel or Crate
Pure hard surface, zero organic shapes. Estimated time: 45–90 mins. Teaches: extrude, inset, bevel, boolean for hardware details. Prints in 1–2 hrs. Sells at $8–12 for a set. Your landscape background means you'll nail the weathered, beaten look immediately. This is your "print and sell it this weekend" piece.
2
Terrain: Modular Dungeon Wall (4" segment)
Block modeling, Mirror modifier, Array modifier. Estimated time: 2–3 hrs. Design it once and print 20. Teaches modularity — the highest-leverage skill for terrain design. Stone brick detail can be achieved with a Displacement modifier or by hand-sculpting a 4×4 section and instancing it with Array. Sells as sets of 4–8 pieces.
3
Character: Simple DnD Monster Mini
Sculpt mode re-entry. Start with something humanoid but beefy — a heavily armored guard, a golem, a troll. Estimated time: 3–5 hrs. Teaches: Dyntopo sculpting, remesh, Decimate modifier for file size. Your character background is your biggest asset here — you'll progress faster than most people starting minis from scratch. Design thick, simple features that survive 32mm scale.
🖨️
K2 Plus specific: When you're ready to plan CFS color splits, do it during the Blender phase, not the slicer phase. Separate your mesh objects by color region before export — e.g., barrel body (brown) as one object, metal bands (grey) as another. Export as separate STLs, then combine in your slicer and assign filament slots. This gives you cleaner color boundaries than painting in the slicer alone.
